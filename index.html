PESOSE Track 1 · Scoping an open-source ecosystem

Security at a price you set.

Almost all software depends on open source, and most of those components carry a known vulnerability. Fixing one has a runtime price. PARAPET turns each exposure into a reviewable decision — measured on the project's own workloads and checked against a budget the maintainer declared in advance.

01 / The problem we scope

Big projects can price a fix. Most cannot.

Mozilla measured the cost of sandboxing the libGraphite font library — about 85% added to that library's execution, but only 3% added latency on real page loads — judged it acceptable, and shipped it in Firefox. The Linux kernel measured its CPU mitigations, found the cost uneven across workloads, and ships a boot switch that turns them off. Both could decide because both had paid engineers, representative workloads, and a benchmarking harness.

A library with two volunteers and a thousand dependents has none of that, so the hardening change that matters most stalls at a contributor who cannot weigh it. PARAPET is a one-year effort to scope an ecosystem where a maintainer states the price once, in public — a service budget on added latency, memory, and CPU — and every proposed hardening change arrives already checked against it, carrying a record of what it removed, what proved it, and what it cost.

03 / Building the ecosystem

From one maintainer's script to shared infrastructure

Track 1 funds planning, not product: the architecture, the governance, the security plan, and the community strategy an exposure-to-decision framework needs before adoption scales.

01

Service-budget authority

A maintainer-written, versioned limit on added latency, memory, and CPU cost, treated as access control: the charter names who may write it, who may change it, and how downstream consumers learn it moved.

02

Trusted measurement

Held-out workloads, a stopping rule and effect size fixed before the run, a recorded environment, and tail-aware reporting on a dedicated quiesced host. The measurement is the newest thing to trust and the cheapest to attack.

03

Agent accountability

Who may operate a contributing agent, how its changes are labeled, and the review they need beyond the human default — each carrying an equivalence check and a hardening record with the evidence.

04

Governance & funding

An Apache-2.0 core weighed against MPL-2.0, a contributor agreement or DCO, and a funding model tested with maintainers, program offices, and public-interest funders including OpenSSF and the Linux Foundation.

04 / The ecosystem

Checked against the record.

PARAPET does not rewrite a project's security code unseen. Every hardening change arrives as a record — what was removed, the check that proves it, the cost, and how to reproduce both — measured against a budget the maintainer wrote.

The ecosystem serves the maintainers who defer hardening they cannot cost, and the operators, program offices, and distributions whose systems depend on those projects. Track 1 produces the plan; a Track 2 award would build the framework itself.
